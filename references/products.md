# Products, claims and accounting boundaries

**Evidence scope:** baseline Core documentation was read September 10, 2026; targeted September 18 product evidence, September 25 Predict/Treasury/Asset Bond evidence and September 26 NET Advance documentation/app text update only the named topics. This is not a fresh audit of every older parameter, contract permission or balance sheet. Publisher rules, app methodology and block-specific observations establish different things. Exact identities and generations are routed by [address-index.json](../assets/address-index.json).

**Questions answered:** What claim does each product create, and who bears its losses? How do Predict trading, House Vault underwriting and retail LP provision differ? Why can deployed contracts or an operating lending desk still have disabled app controls? Which product assets and fees actually enter Core backing?

## Standard bonds, staking and the concluded founding offering

Standard Primary Offerings accept USDG or canonical v2 LP and price NET at `max(discounted TWAP, NAV)`. Capacity limits apply. LP consideration uses the Treasury's floor-valued geometric-mean convention, not a spot-marked NET leg. The bond claim and the later staking claim are distinct: buying a vesting NET claim is not itself a promise of staking distributions during the vest. The RWA page describes the standard and RWA bond vest as two-day linear delivery. [Mechanism §5](https://docs.netnet.capital/mechanism), [RWA Desk](https://docs.netnet.capital/rwa-desk).

Staking converts NET into sNET; its balance grows with successful rebases. wsNET is a non-rebasing wrapper whose underlying claim changes with the index and can be posted as Loopback collateral. Do not add the wrapper and its backing as independent holdings, or assume a growing index guarantees a profitable dollar exit. The founding guide describes a zero-epoch warmup as a default and tells readers to consult the deployed value; it does not establish a timeless warmup for every deployment. [Mechanism §§1,6](https://docs.netnet.capital/mechanism), [founding guide](https://docs.netnet.capital/founding-shareholder-guide).

**GenesisBond is a separate, concluded product.** The offering and guide report finalization on **2026-07-16**, full subscription and completion of the five-day founding vest. Finalization atomically formed the Treasury/canonical POL, enabled Core programs and started the fee/pTEAM clocks. A founder's nontransferable certificate records cohort membership; no launch perk is promised in code. Unclaimed vested founding NET is documented not to expire, but this is not a reopened offering. Old funding/subscription steps remain as history, not current availability instructions. All binding historical prices, limits, ratios and vesting terms are on the [Founding Offering](https://docs.netnet.capital/OFFERING.HTM); the [guide](https://docs.netnet.capital/founding-shareholder-guide) is subordinate explanatory history.

## Retail NET/USDG v2 liquidity and the LP Zap

**September 18 availability:** the [application](https://app.netnet.capital/#/invest), **Invest → Shareholder Desk → Add liquidity**, displays a direct Uniswap alternative using NET and USDG. Its custom USDG-only, one-transaction **LP Zap is deployed but app-gated**, not an enabled tax-free retail route. The [indexed creation transaction](https://robin.etherscan.io/tx/0xf554b130e97fc10e63384c6d685e101a1b80916adbcb51044f200c3445031d1b) succeeded on September 16; the [September 18 app bundle](https://app.netnet.capital/assets/index-9OJ6-wtv.js) still marks the Zap `PLACEHOLDER` and omits its live capability. The visible panel says the desk opens once its trading-levy exemption executes. This establishes deployment and interface gating, **not a fresh on-chain exemption check**; registry promotion and exemption execution are separate evidence questions.

The gated design spends part of a USDG input on NET and deposits both assets at the canonical **Uniswap v2** pool ratio. The receipt is a fungible share of that pool, not sNET, wsNET, nnUSDG, a v3 position NFT or a Predict House Vault share. The UI's approximate “USDG value of the pool” is a valuation, not a stablecoin repayment promise. A rising NET price generally leaves an LP with less NET and more USDG than the original mix; a falling price does the reverse. This is **unstaked NET exposure**: provision itself creates no sNET rebase entitlement.

Keep three costs distinct:

- The app describes the pool's **0.3% swap fee** as LP income. In standard v2 accounting it accrues in pool reserves, not a separate v3 NFT collect balance. It is not a guaranteed APR or net profit.
- The [NET fee schedule](https://docs.netnet.capital/FEES.HTM) specifies a **5% token transfer levy** on mapped-pair transfers, separately routed through TaxCollector—not paid to LPs as their pool fee. The visible direct-Uniswap fallback warns that the NET leg pays that levy. Protocol-owned-liquidity exemptions do not automatically exempt retail users.
- The gated form has a dynamic warning that the levy can apply on **two NET legs** before the Zap's exemption, despite unconditional lower copy saying “Adding pays no levy.” Treat that latter wording as the intended exempt path, not a current guarantee. Two sequential NET-leg levies are not a flat 10% charge on the entire USDG deposit. Swap price impact, slippage and execution costs remain even with an exemption; no independently verified all-in Zap fee schedule is supplied.

The app describes exit through **Uniswap**, returning both tokens at the prevailing ratio and warning of a levy on the NET portion. “Redeems at any time” does not guarantee original units, dollar principal, liquidity, uninterrupted execution or a tax-free exit. Divergence loss relative to holding the initial mix can exceed fees; NET volatility, USDG depeg, pool/contract defects and entry/exit costs can reduce principal. Spot-valued retail LP holdings are not Core's floor-valued POL RFV. For the separate Sleeve v3 accounting method, see [LP fee inspection](lp-fee-inspection.md); for packaged pool and Zap identities use the [address index](../assets/address-index.json). Explain evidenced user-operated liquidity steps, including route, inputs, approvals, confirmation and exit costs, when asked. Under the [guardrail](guardrail.md), the agent must not perform them or prepare ready-to-sign/submit artifacts.

For current provider holdings, capital flows and historical fee attribution, use [v2 liquidity analytics](liquidity-analytics.md). It separates unknown owners from evidenced third parties and gross assessed swap fees from protocol dilution, net retention and realized cash.

### Trader claim, prices and dynamic fees

USDG buys one series' **HIGHER or LOWER ERC-20 tokens**. A winning token redeems for **1 USDG**, the loser for zero; USDG is not guaranteed USD. Selling before close uses the dealer quote, not original cost. The documented dealer curve uses fixed weekly funding plus a 10% virtual buffer, steepness 0.60 of that backing and complementary marks. Prices respond to book imbalance, not an external probability oracle. The docs' broad “round trip can never profit” language is not a guarantee against price changes caused by intervening traders.

The documented swap fee is **3% at balance**, rising to **6% for skew-adding flow** or falling to **1.5% for skew-reducing flow** at the cap. Buys must reduce skew or leave it within **35% of backing**; sells remain available while trading is open and can carry imbalance above the buy cap. Thus the quoted roughly one-seventh House loss at the cap is a scenario, not a universal maximum. On-chain/UI minimums are documented as **1/5 USDG**; slippage tolerance is separate from the fee.

Half the fee goes to the House's series bucket; half buys NET through the taxed canonical pair for **Sleeve RWA inventory**, not burning. If the NET buy fails, the trade may complete with USDG deferred to the next buy. Therefore assessed fees, executed NET purchases, Sleeve receipts and Core TaxCollector remittances are separate accounting legs. A September 18 [funded trade](https://robin.etherscan.io/tx/0x1b52872e4a623776abb679d73a29e79bb3da7759ac202deda49f3408b6e160e3) corroborates a purchase route, not every fee split or all-time revenue.

### Trading close, settlement and void limits

Documented UTC schedule: open Monday from **14:00** (late weekday openings allowed with at least six hours before close), last call Saturday **02:00**, close Saturday **04:00**, print Monday **00:00**, Safe grade afterward. Last call permits only skew-reducing buys; sells continue until close. Use each series' actual timestamps. The Safe posts the print; **strictly above the line wins HIGHER, equality wins LOWER**. If ungraded **72 hours after print**, anyone may void at the final complementary trading marks—not purchase-cost refunds.

Opening guards require at least **50,000 USDG** in the vault, a prior grade/void followed by a six-hour turn, line within **75–150%** of the current number and that number at least **90%** of the Treasury view. Grade bounds are at least half the line and 90% of live Treasury, and at most twice the greater of line/Treasury. These are documented range checks, **not authentication of a correct Sleeve mark**. Current implementation and authority still require matching evidence.

**Settlement trust is central:** the documented team Safe/grader supplies the print; an in-range print is not necessarily a correct one. Current grader appointment powers, the full privileged-control set and independent rights to dispute a posted print are unverified without matching implementation/authority evidence. A dated audit showing equal role addresses is not proof of current ownership. The bundled analytics ABI intentionally omits writes: absence of a challenge function there is **not evidence that challenge rights do not exist**. The documented 72-hour ungraded void addresses a **missing grade**, not an incorrect posted print.

The separate closest-guess contest requires **3 USDG of series fees**, one whole-dollar guess per wallet before close, highest guess not over the print, with ties to the earlier guess. Permissionless proposals have a **24-hour challenge window**; the prize is **10% of the vault's half of fees**, rolling over if every guess exceeds the print.

That **prize-winner challenge is not a challenge to the settlement print**. See the [dated source and control review](predict-analytics.md#settlement-authority-and-distinct-remedies). The September 25 live frontend amount model rejects *increases* in absolute skew at last call, permitting equality in that model, whereas publisher prose says “skew-reducing”; exact deployed strictness remains unverified. Neither prose nor frontend constants establish a live quote or trade acceptance. Use the [read-only quote research recipe](predict-analytics.md#short-read-only-quote-research-recipe); the helper's `predict --series ID` is a narrow snapshot, not a quote engine.

### House Vault: underwriting, not passive savings

House depositors supply USDG as loss-bearing counterparty capital. Shares are **internal, nontransferable balances**, one pro-rata class; the fund receives no preference or first-loss obligation. The vault's accounting is not its raw token balance. It is not AMM liquidity, nnUSDG lending or Core-guaranteed savings.

**Pending deposits** back nothing and earn/lose nothing until the next turn; they are cancellable before conversion. **Withdrawal notices** keep backing the book until the print of the next series opened after notice, then become set-aside claims; notice is cancellable until paid. **Turn order:** reserve winners first, return residual desk assets plus net House fees, strike share price, set aside matured withdrawals, then convert pending deposits. Unredeemed winners' reservations do not expire. Shares, pending cash, active assets and matured claims must not be counted twice or treated as equally liquid.

The docs report the first struck share price as **1.041261 USDG per initial-dollar share**: an attributed historical weekly result, not forecast APR or independently reconciled withdrawals. Team Safe, Sleeve and keeper are documented blocked from buying; a halt affects buys, not sells within the clock, grade, redemption, void or vault functions. These are product-specific rules, not proof of current enforcement or absence of Safe grading/liveness risk.

Use [Predict and House analytics](predict-analytics.md) for series activity, transfer reconciliation and ownership/queues; use `rfv --scope reports` for the separate current Sleeve memo. Website figures never supply live numerical inputs. The agent may explain user-operated trading, deposit and withdrawal steps, but must not perform wallet actions. **18+; a bet, not a hedge.**

## Real World Bonds and the RWA Sleeve

### Historical documented economic flow

The older published Desk design distributes NET that the Manager has already acquired through pTEAM exercise and deposited as Desk inventory. This is a **generation-scoped description**, not proof of the September 12 bought-NET path's mechanics. A subscription does **not itself create fresh NET supply** merely because it creates a vesting note. The earlier exercise and later distribution are separate accounting events. In that documented design, subscriber USDG splits between:

1. **Reserve remittance to Treasury**, described per NET as high-water backing minus the 1 USDG strike already contributed at exercise.
2. **Rialto equity acquisition**, with the stock held in the team-custodied RWA Sleeve.
3. **Subscriber NET claim**, delivered from Desk inventory over the published two-day linear vest.

The subscriber does not receive the purchased equity and has no documented proportional Sleeve redemption right. Do not book the whole USDG subscription as Core revenue, count an inventory transfer as a new mint, or count the earlier pTEAM strike twice. [RWA Desk](https://docs.netnet.capital/rwa-desk), [team](https://docs.netnet.capital/team), [Treasury §5](https://docs.netnet.capital/treasury).

### Prices, controls and inventory

The following pricing, remittance and inventory controls describe the older published Desk design. Match them to a generation before treating them as present terms; the September 12 announcement does not restate or certify them for bought inventory.

The documented Desk price is discounted TWAP. The launch discount is an historical configurable parameter; the published immutable upper bound is **7.5%**. A high-water-backing premium guard is documented at **1.20×**. The page alternates between saying subscriptions halt below the guard and saying the higher of price/floor binds; do not infer which deployed quoting/revert behavior resolves that wording without generation-specific evidence. A displayed launch “all-in advantage” is not a timeless return: vesting exposure, current discount, oracle state, inventory and the actual alternative trade's fee/slippage matter. [RWA Desk price and risk sections](https://docs.netnet.capital/rwa-desk).

The reserve remittance follows a monotone high-water backing mark. The page's precise claim is that **a fully sold inventory clip restores backing to its pre-Desk high-water mark**. Do not reinterpret this as proving that pre-exercise NAV cannot temporarily fall: pTEAM exercise is itself a documented NAV-diluting exception, and inventory may remain unsold. Report exercise, remittance, remaining inventory and the relevant high-water mark separately. The published inventory design has no Manager withdrawal path: its exit is a filled vesting claim. This is an inventory-custody statement, **not proof that recipients can never subsequently sell NET** or that there is no economic sell pressure. [RWA Desk reserve remittance and inventory](https://docs.netnet.capital/rwa-desk), [mechanism §4](https://docs.netnet.capital/mechanism).

The menu consists of token/feed/target-weight entries. The documentation names NVDA, SPCX and AAPL as launch names and says the Manager can change names and weights; removal stops future purchases, not sale of existing holdings. It also says both that subscribers choose an equity and that each fill buys the most-underweight name. Treat the exact selection algorithm as unresolved in this summary, rather than pretending the two statements prove buyer discretion. Price/feed liveness, Rialto execution and the backing guard can halt new subscriptions without cancelling already-issued vesting notes. [RWA Desk menu and risks](https://docs.netnet.capital/rwa-desk).

### Sleeve ownership, claims and risks

The Sleeve is a dedicated, team-custodied Safe holding equities for the Manager's stated protocol-benefit mandate. Other programs can forward stocks not present on the Desk's purchase menu. No Sleeve asset counts in RFV or NAV; no Treasury reserve path is described as funding those equity purchases. Possible use to support backing or inverse bonds is **discretionary and social**, not automatic collateral or an enforceable cash redemption facility. [RWA Desk Sleeve](https://docs.netnet.capital/rwa-desk), [Treasury §§5–6](https://docs.netnet.capital/treasury).

The Sleeve is also Credit's documented principal Stock Token borrower: gross marked equity is not necessarily unencumbered net value. Distinguish custody, collateral, USDG debt, interest and withdrawability; a stock gain affects Core NAV only through a separately evidenced Core transfer. Equity/issuer/redemption, feed, market-hours and team-custody risks remain. [Credit](https://docs.netnet.capital/credit), [risks §12](https://docs.netnet.capital/risks). See Strategy for [full Sleeve/social-support accounting](rwa-strategy.md#the-non-negotiable-accounting-boundary) and the [combined-assets forecast](rwa-strategy.md#september-11-forecast-treasury-plus-manager-sleeve-not-core-rfv).

## Managed Futures: real transactions, test economics

The documented mainnet test venue has SPCX and CASHCAT markets. Its margin currency **tNET is a freely mintable, valueless faucet token**, not NET, wsNET, a Treasury claim or a production stablecoin. SPCX uses an equity feed; CASHCAT uses a v3 TWAP combined with ETH/USD. Mainnet deployment and real gas expenditure do not make test PnL monetary revenue. The stated production path—real wsNET margin, a strategic underwriting raise and fees routed to Treasury—is expressly **not live** in the read documentation. [Futures §§1–2,9](https://docs.netnet.capital/futures), [official registry](https://docs.netnet.capital/official-channels).

For test risk calculations, tNET is marked as if it were wsNET using the Core TWAP and staking index. The documented usable-margin rule clamps a haircut price between an indexed NAV floor and twice that floor; actual PnL settlement uses the contemporaneous modeled spot conversion. This is a simulated valuation convention, not an actual floor redemption promise for tNET. The venue documents leverage, maintenance margin, per-notional open/close fees and skew-driven eight-hour carry; carry flows from the crowded to the less-crowded side, with a fee-route skim. These parameters describe test mechanics, not investable yield. [Futures §§1,3](https://docs.netnet.capital/futures).

Underwriters deposit tNET and hold vault shares whose value varies with the vault's counterparty equity. The documented loss waterfall is trader margin → underwriting vault → clearing reserve → auto-deleveraging profitable opposing positions. Underwriting redemptions can be gated while the book needs capital, and a drawdown breaker can halt new positions. **Treasury is not a loss layer**: the test desk's Core connections are read-only, and its fees terminate in a test-token sink rather than Core. [Futures §§4–6](https://docs.netnet.capital/futures).

Out-of-session or between-print equity indices can stay unchanged while economic exposure accumulates; a reopening gap can trigger margin calls at once. Staleness halts new risk according to the published guard, not all possible loss. Test traders can be liquidated and underwriters can lose their test balance; the risk lesson carries over even though tNET has no monetary value. [Futures §§2,8 and risk disclosures](https://docs.netnet.capital/futures).

## Loopback: isolated wsNET/USDG credit

Direct public entry: [Loopback application](https://app.netnet.capital/#/loopback). Explain evidenced user-operated borrowing and looping steps when asked, including collateral units, debt, approvals, target leverage, taxes and liquidation risks. The agent must not borrow, run the looping router or perform wallet operations.

Loopback is documented as operating since **2026-07-22** on an immutable Morpho market. Borrowers post wsNET, retain its index-linked staking exposure and incur floating USDG debt. Direct USDG lenders fund the loans; Morpho collateral is described as not re-lent. **Core Treasury is not a credit participant or loan guarantor.** This separation concerns Loopback participant loans, not the Treasury's independently described Morpho reserve deployment. [Loopback](https://docs.netnet.capital/lending), [Treasury §2](https://docs.netnet.capital/treasury).

The published oracle credits collateral at:

```text
credited USDG value per wsNET = clamp(TWAP × 0.90, NAV, 5 × NAV) × index
liquidation LLTV = 62.5% of credited collateral value
Loopback Turbo router target = 53.125%
```

The optional leveraged accumulation flow uses borrowed USDG to buy NET, stake and wrap it, and return wsNET as collateral. It pays the normal mapped-pool trading tax and slippage; leveraged dividend exposure is not free leverage or guaranteed carry profit. This **Loopback TurboRouter is not the Long-Dated TURBO desk or TURBO BLACKJACK**. [Loopback terms and fee alignment](https://docs.netnet.capital/lending), [official registry](https://docs.netnet.capital/official-channels).

The five-times-NAV cap restrains credit against large speculative premiums. While the cap binds, some price declines do not change credited value; this does **not** mean market-price crashes cannot ever cause liquidation. A sufficient fall leaves the capped region, while debt interest, NAV dilution and the staking index also change health. The lower NAV clamp can value collateral above executable market price when NET trades below NAV, despite the page's “at or below market” prose. Liquidation recovery depends on actual buyers and capacity, not merely the credited mark. [Loopback advance rate and margin calls](https://docs.netnet.capital/lending), [risks §§1,4,14](https://docs.netnet.capital/risks).

A stale TWAP or instantaneous pool price more than 15% below TWAP is documented to pause new borrowing **and liquidations**. A frozen oracle is not a loss guarantee: gaps and illiquidity can worsen while liquidators cannot act. Debt above the permitted credited-value threshold is subject to permissionless liquidation with Morpho's incentive; rates float with utilization. The page's facility-size guidance relative to pool depth is explicitly **not an enforced cap**. USDG lenders can lose principal; rate-limited inverse bonds are not an instant liquidation backstop. [Loopback stale guard, margin calls and lender risks](https://docs.netnet.capital/lending).

### Bytecode-backed mechanics and their qualifications

The analyzed Desk enforces a **30-day term** and a **40% advance at the open mark**, rounded down. For actual advance `A`, integer USDG-unit arithmetic is `owed = floor(A × 11500 / 10000)` and Treasury reserve `T = floor(A × 500 / 10000)`; the quoted House fee is the residual `owed − A − T`, not invariably a separately floored 10%. Opening requires unallocated capacity **at least `A + T`**: the user destination receives all `A`, while `T` moves from unallocated cash to escrow, not immediately to Treasury. The open mark uses the lower of oracle TWAP and nonzero pool spot, scaled by the current index (zero spot falls back to oracle). The actual close mark uses oracle TWAP and the current index after a bounded staking catch-up attempt. These are generation-scoped rules, not a universal payout independent of caps, minimums, conversion costs, rounding or transaction-time dependency responses.

The lock holds fixed wsNET units, not a rebasing token. For a matured unpaid position, let `W` be locked wsNET raw units, `U = owed − paid`, and `M` the usable close mark in USDG raw units per whole wsNET. Actual close takes `min(W, ceil(U × 10^18 / M))` wsNET and credits `min(U, floor(takenWs × M / 10^18))` to `paid`. `TakeSplit` attributes that take to yield first and principal second; it does not limit the aggregate take to yield. **All principal can be consumed**, and an uncovered remainder emits `Shortfall` while the position still closes. Taken wsNET stays in the Desk as House inventory; the residual transfers to the recorded owner. Close does not sell it on an AMM or collect USDG: **`paid` combines actual USDG repayments with collateral valuation credit**, and `Closed.usdgCovered` is only that close's increment.

Anyone may repay USDG before maturity, including while halted; only the lesser of requested cash and the remaining balance is pulled. Partial repayment releases no collateral. Full repayment immediately finalizes and returns the entire lock to its **recorded owner**, not the payer or advance destination, including for third-party repayment. An unpaid close requires maturity but then permits any caller. **Maturity is not automatic settlement**: a transaction, usable dependencies and successful transfers are still required.

At finalization the exact Treasury entitlement is **`due = min(T, floor(T × finalPaid / owed))`**. Escrow releases all `T`: `T − due` returns to House-unallocated USDG and `due` is attempted from the reserved cash, even when recovery is entirely collateral-based. Full repayment/coverage earns all `T`; shortfall proportionally reduces it. A reverting USDG Treasury transfer or canonical `false` is deferred in `treasuryOwed`, allowing finalization to continue; a successful call returning malformed/empty boolean data reverts the whole call. Owner collateral-return failures also revert. Thus neither “Treasury failures never block unlock” nor “fee entitlement proves remittance” is valid.

The quoted **approximately 16% price cushion** is conditional on a particular September 23 dividend-rate illustration. It is neither an immutable threshold nor principal protection: lower or zero rebases can consume principal with a much smaller price fall. The page's roughly 70% full-lock exhaustion example is conditional too. Spending dilution-based rebases can preserve a token count while reducing its share of total supply. Do not reproduce historical price, rate or portfolio examples as live inputs or promised returns.

The docs say stale TWAP blocks marked opens, inventory sales and unpaid closes; actual oracle freshness/acceptance remains dependency evidence, not established by Desk bytecode alone. Repay and fully paid finalization need no price mark. The analyzed halt gates new opens/inventory purchases, not repay/close. `previewClose` uses **projectedIndex**, whereas actual close uses the index after bounded catch-up; a failed/no-progress rebase can end catch-up early, and eight advancing but still-due epochs can revert. A preview neither checks maturity nor proves executable settlement. The House's “no open-term price exposure” claim assumes full marked coverage; shortfall, oracle, realizability and contract risks remain.

### Advance scenarios: user and protocol together

Answer ordinary “what if” questions directly. Interpret “advance 10 NET” as locking 10 NET-equivalent unless context indicates the desired USDG payout; name that interpretation briefly. Default to the applicable contract/position term and an unchanged evidenced per-epoch staking/index growth rate, no voluntary repayment, and retention of the advanced USDG without reinvestment. Use a documented term only as an explicitly qualified fallback. User-specified hypothetical changes override scenario defaults, not observed contract behavior. Do not request wallet details for hypothetical arithmetic. Explain material costs as exclusions when unknown; a cost-excluded result is not net realized profit.

For a constant holder/index growth rate `r` across `n` successful modeled epochs, use `G = (1+r)^n`; for supplied index endpoints use `G = I1/I0`. Scheduled epochs need not actually execute. “Maximum issuance” is not automatically `r`: establish the emission denominator, staking participation, distribution/index conversion and relevant caps. A scenario may explicitly assume the maximum remains feasible, but must not present that assumption as a guarantee or invent the holder-growth mapping. If the mapping or current rate is missing, calculate what is determined and leave growth symbolic or provide an explicitly hypothetical sensitivity, rather than blocking the whole answer.

**Derive outcomes from applicable contract mechanics, not a prose-only recipe.** Use the bytecode-backed integer rules above for this evidenced generation, then establish fresh position/state applicability where needed. Frontend ABIs alone do not establish enforcement; explorer runtime analysis does not establish current balances, dependency behavior or a transaction's outcome. If current implementation evidence differs, use it and disclose the difference. Do not downgrade the established Treasury shortfall allocation to “unknown” merely because current rates or positions are unavailable; those are separate inputs.

The following is a continuous-unit approximation to those settlement rules, not the exact integer calculation. Assume no interim repayments, a feasible full advance, positive applicable marks, and settlement at the modeled closing mark. Let `Q` be starting NET-equivalent, `P0` its starting valuation, `Po` the applicable opening mark per NET-equivalent, and `P1` the closing mark. Opening and starting marks need not agree. Let advance fraction `a`, House fee fraction `h`, and Treasury fraction `t` be applicable evidenced terms; any changed terms or growth rates must be explicit hypothetical assumptions:

```text
A = a × Q × Po                 USDG advanced
D = A × (1+h+t)                modeled balance to cover
V = Q × G × P1                 closing lock value before settlement
S = min(D, V)                  settlement value taken, capped by collateral
shortfall = D − S
returned_NET_equivalent = (V − S) / P1
user_ending_value = A + V − S  if advanced USDG is retained
user_marked_gain = user_ending_value − Q × P0
staking_without_Advance_value = V
user_difference_vs_staking = A − S
combined_House_Treasury_net_value = S − A
```

Keep marked collateral and cash distinct. Their continuous-model conservation check is `user_marked_gain + combined_House_Treasury_net_value = V − Q×P0`, before costs and other flows. Under full coverage, gross nominal fees are `(h+t)×A`; recovered `A` is principal, not revenue. Under shortfall, use the exact floored Treasury formula above (approximately `t×A×S/D` without repayments), not a full-coverage split or a Treasury-first recovery waterfall. House marked net is collateral recovery plus cash repayment, less `A` and Treasury entitlement; the unused Treasury reserve is its own cash returned, not extra revenue. Integer take-rounding can leave collateral valuation dust above credited coverage. These allocations are not proof of liquid sale proceeds or observed remittance.

If the advance is spent, remove it from ending financial assets while reporting it separately as previously received/consumed value; do not imply spending made the protocol seize more collateral. If reinvested, replace retained cash with the modeled investment proceeds and costs. If voluntarily repaid or closed early, use the applicable cash-flow and settlement rules rather than deducting the full maturity balance again. Returned wsNET/NET-equivalent is the user's residual, not an additional House asset.

For the protocol, distinguish the House/RWA Sleeve from Core Treasury. Pledged collateral is not House equity; a receivable, its collateral and its eventual recovery cannot all be added as separate gains. Treasury fee entitlement or escrow is not cash already remitted to Core. A separately established marginal Treasury receipt may affect RFV, but total RFV and per-token NAV also require the applicable reserve accounting and supply path. Maximum issuance can grow the user's token count while diluting supply share or per-token backing; do not infer other holders' returns or NAV growth solely from that user's gain or a positive Treasury fee. Give a concise user outcome plus relevant protocol consequences, not a mandatory multi-section template.
