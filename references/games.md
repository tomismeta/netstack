## Accounting and security distinctions

1. **Player expected value is not holder profit.** A payout multiple is normally gross payout including the returned stake, not net profit. A negative game expectation does not identify which party earns the difference. Separate Manager house P&L, fee buckets, stock purchases, Treasury remittances, and burns. Do not add them together as “protocol revenue.”
2. **The RWA Sleeve is not Core backing.** The cited [RWA disclosure](https://docs.netnet.capital/rwa-desk) and game pages describe Manager custody and discretionary support—not Treasury RFV, automatic buybacks, NET-holder redemption rights or guaranteed distributions. A game's local “treasury” or USDG float is not the protocol Treasury. See [Strategy's full accounting boundary](rwa-strategy.md#the-non-negotiable-accounting-boundary).
3. **A burn is a supply event, not cash revenue.** WinNET's burn removes NET while reserves are unchanged, which mechanically improves backing per remaining NET under the documented accounting. It is not a dollar paid to holders. Rebases increase token quantities and should not be relabeled external cash yield.
4. **Core-linked does not mean Core-controlled or Core-guaranteed.** WinNET and CLIMB compose as ordinary holders; Superstore distributes Manager pTEAM-derived inventory and remits backing-related USDG. Their docs deny permissions over Core funds. The equity-native arcade desks explicitly do not involve NET, the canonical pair, or reserve-backed wagering. Their Manager halts and operating parameters must not be confused with immutable Core policy.
5. **Verifiable randomness is not favorable odds or complete trustlessness.** Most simple cabinets use future drand quicknet rounds with on-chain BLS signature verification, not a generic claim of “Chainlink VRF.” A relay is still needed. Blackjack needs a hidden committed shoe and an off-chain state channel. Board Meeting's table outcome is human choice; BASKETS has a signed referee result, distinct from its random drop schedule and jackpot. Dial-Up/Runner use house-signed market reports for price outcomes. State these differences explicitly.
6. **Stock units and dollar values differ.** Matching liabilities and bankroll in the same stock token helps unit solvency; it does not preserve USDG value or eliminate token-issuer, redemption, oracle, contract, or execution risk. A stock sale queued for reopening is not a locked dollar payout.

**Arithmetic discrepancy to preserve:** Invaders and Flight Simulator both print a 90% board, a fee of 5% of stake, `stake = all-in / 1.05`, and combined return “about 85.5%.” Those stated mechanics imply `0.90 / 1.05 = 85.714…%` before conversion costs, not `0.90 × 0.95 = 85.5%`. Quote the documentation's figure as a claim and expose the denominator mismatch; do not silently resolve it as verified deployment behavior. COINflip's corresponding `1 / 1.05 = 95.238…%` agrees with its rounded 95.2% disclosure.

## WinNET — pooled staking, not a cash-preserving lottery

Primary source: [Prize Draw Program](https://docs.netnet.capital/winnet). Documentation says operating with nightly draws; availability and balances still require fresh evidence.

- Each depositor owns a **fixed quantity of NET**. The pooled vault stakes normally. The prize is the vault's staked balance above total fixed principal claims, allocated every three eight-hour dividend epochs. Principal does not individually accrue the ordinary staking return absent a prize; its dollar value moves with NET.
- One winner receives the pool less a **5% unstaked NET burn**, credited to their principal and subject to a new one-draw commitment for that prize tranche. A post-burn prize below **50 USDG-equivalent at the NAV floor** rolls over. A premium-driven dividend rate of zero can produce no prize.
- Selection weight is real principal plus active **Bonus Draw Balance**. Bonus is nonredeemable odds weight, inert without principal, and expires after 14 draws. Five consolation selections excluding the main winner receive $25-equivalent bonus weight, not cash or NET.
- The source lists fee credit-back, check-ins, streaks, referrals, and 7/30-draw lock boosts. “Fee credit-back” is bonus weight, not a refunded trading fee. It explicitly says welcome-credit/match grants were defined but had not begun; do not infer they are active now. Bonus-key compromise can skew odds within a stated per-draw budget, not directly withdraw funds.
- USDG entry and USDG exit traverse the ordinary taxed canonical pair (5% each leg plus impact); direct NET deposits omit the buy leg and fee credit. Every deposit has a first-draw commitment of roughly one day. Early release of an optional longer lock forfeits boosts/streak credits and adds **1% principal to the prize pool**. Thus the page's “dividends only” funding description has this explicit penalty contribution as a qualification.
- drand is named at cutoff, weighted selection is deterministic from on-chain weights and the verified beacon, and no fallback seed is used. Missing relay by the deadline skips the draw and rolls the pool intact. A relayer can withhold an observed unfavorable draw, but relay is permissionless; this is a liveness limitation, not authority to select another winner.
- There is no Treasury, RFV, or protocol-owned-liquidity prize guarantee. “NET count preserved” is not “no loss”: market decline, entry/exit tax, pool impact, locks, foregone individual accrual, and contract failures remain material.

**Historical incentive conflict:** the [July 27 launch](https://x.com/NetNetCap/status/2081835116326989902) announced WinNET live; its [incentive post](https://x.com/NetNetCap/status/2081835121272057915) advertised $5 welcome draw balance, $10 with a friend's code and additional first-deposit credit. The [July 28 reminder](https://x.com/NetNetCap/status/2082167668493488408) offered $10 plus 10% of the first deposit. Yet docs read September 10 say welcome/match grants had not begun: payment and present availability remain unverified, with the chronology unresolved. Credit is **draw weight, not withdrawable cash**; “free money,” “no loss” and “risk nothing” do not override the risks above. See [Privy/email and sponsored-gas onboarding](integrations.md#privy-historical-winnet-onboarding), not permission to log in or play.

## CLIMB, INC. — escrowed assets behind a corporate-life game

Primary source: [Corporate Ladder Program](https://docs.netnet.capital/climb). Documented opening: 2026-08-05.

Advancement purchases are real positions, not consumed virtual-content fees. USDG buys NET through the 5%-taxed canonical pool and stakes it, or buys tokenized equities through Rialto. The escrowed book's total marked value gates promotion, with a documented $1,000 Chairman's-floor gate. Staked NET continues accruing rebases.

The three economic exits must stay distinct:

- **Resignation:** 80% of the then-existing book returns in kind; 20% enters the jackpot pool.
- **Termination:** the player loses the entire book. NET goes to the jackpot; equities go to the Manager's RWA Sleeve. “Everything lost goes to the jackpot” is therefore inaccurate.
- **Win:** the full book returns together with **50% of the jackpot pool**; the other half stays for later winners. The pool takes no house rake; the Manager may reclaim only its own unspent USDG seed, not player contributions. The page's $20,000 seed is historical, not today's prize.

The Shareholders' Meeting grants three verified endgame rolls. The ante has an on-chain floor of **5% of the current jackpot**; base jackpot chance is **2% per roll**, with protocol-activity boosts up to a hard cap. The page names WinNET, Superstore, Loopback, outside-game staked NET, and career history as inputs but does **not give the numerical maximum odds cap or complete activity formula**. Do not calculate a personalized chance from this reference. Deposits are sealed while seated; exhausting the rolls triggers standard forfeiture. drand supplies the verified outcomes.

Most full climbs are expected to lose the book according to the disclosure. A $1,000 promotion gate is not a fixed all-in price for the endgame, and low odds plus a growing ante are not a guaranteed arbitrage on the jackpot. NET/equity prices can fall while escrowed; released NET sold for USDG pays ordinary sell tax. The desk/pool are additional contracts, not Treasury liabilities. Complete health/reputation transitions, activity boosts, and current deployment rules require a more specific current primary source.

## Superstore — randomized NET inventory with an equity election

Controlling mechanics: [Superstore](https://docs.netnet.capital/superstore). Consumer-facing terms: [Superstore Store Policy](https://docs.netnet.capital/superstore-store-policy). Both were read; documented opening is 2026-07-30.

A USDG box names an Edition and an equity from the active menu. Launch Editions cost **$10/$100/$500/$1,000** and have floors **0.5×/0.6×/0.7×/0.8×**. The launch menu is MSFT, AAPL, NVDA, GOOGL; signature cover art does not force that equity choice. The launch headline odds/prizes are respectively $1,000 at 1/2,000, $2,500 at 1/1,000, $5,000 at 1/250, and $5,000 at 1/200. All are conversion-mark examples, not dollar guarantees.

**What is sold:** a fixed random NET quantity from Manager pre-exercised pTEAM inventory. Each price-multiple rung converts at purchase using `max(pair TWAP, pair spot)`. The purchased box snapshots its ladder version, NET amounts, fee basis, equity, election terms, and future drand round. The launch ladders average **90% of box price at that mark before exits**; the floor is the most likely result in every Edition, at least 72.5%. The exact ladders are in the source, not implied by the headline odds alone. **90% is settable, not a minimum enforced by the contract.** The validation checks a table against its declared target, not against an immutable generosity floor. New tables are forward-only and a stale ladder version is rejected at purchase.

**Reveal and election:** a deployed four-second future-beacon delay precedes reveal; the signature is checked on chain. A reveal reimbursement is 20 basis points of price, with a gas-covering floor. The buyer has **60 seconds** after reveal to keep or uninstall; expiry auto-keeps. First purchases have ordinary odds, not seeded wins. The demonstration disc has no stake and pays no prize.

- **Keep:** the full fixed NET prize belongs to the buyer. Under $100 locked prize value is immediate; $100 to under $1,000 vests one day; $1,000 upward vests two days. Vested unclaimed NET does not expire. NET market exposure continues through vesting, and an eventual canonical-pair sale pays ordinary tax and impact. The source's roughly 88% realizable-headline illustration is not a fixed liquidation ratio.
- **Uninstall:** take the named stock instead, at **85% of win for ≤1×**, **80% for >1× to 2×**, or **70% for >2×**. The box's own purchased-equity escrow pays first. A Manager-funded USDG buffer may buy the shortfall, but coverage is finite, first-come-first-served and soft-reserved only at reveal. Once reserved, that slice cannot be withdrawn beneath the winner. **Unreserved future coverage is not guaranteed.** Largest jackpots can spend the entire price on backing remittance and have no equity escrow; their uninstall is buffer-only. The prize NET returns to desk inventory, not a NET burn.

**Routes and backing:** payment covers reveal reimbursement, a backing-related Treasury fee, and equity. Kept-box equity and eligible residuals go to the Sleeve; uninstalled equity goes to the buyer. The owed remittance is `NET payout × (high-water backing per NET − 1 USDG)`, reflecting the Manager's 1-USDG exercise contribution. The higher purchase/settlement high-water mark applies. When fee owed exceeds available payment, a published debt ledger carries the shortfall; its stated launch cap is **$8,250** and affected new purchases halt if they could extend debt beyond it. Uninstalled held fee slices repay that ledger before refilling the buffer.

This is **bounded backing neutrality**, not instantaneous perfect neutrality: outstanding fee debt and pre-exercised unsold inventory can temporarily reduce backing per token. Source inventory settings are 1,000 NET under a 2,000 NET hard cap, with joint bounds across Superstore and Real World Bonds. Neither the 10% ladder shortfall nor gross box sales equals Treasury revenue; they also finance inventory distribution, desk liabilities, equity and delivery.

**Failure paths and current-rule caveats:** an unrevealed box can be buyer-refunded in full after two hours, but not once the signature is registered; no outsider can cancel a visible jackpot. A market-closed/unexecuted equity leg does not cancel the revealed win or delay the election. After 24 hours from reveal, a buyer-elected USDG settlement is available, never automatic; uninstall remains capped by held funds plus its reserved buffer. The policy loosely says public randomness blocks refund, while the mechanics specify registration on chain: distinguish publication from registry state when analyzing a real box. The docs also contain broad stale-feed purchase-halting language alongside weekend purchase examples; exact current buy/settlement gating needs deployment-specific confirmation. A 1.20× high-water-backing premium guard, inventory availability, eight-open-box account limit, and new-sales halt can restrict entry. None establishes today's shelf stock.

## COINflip, SPACEX INVADERS, and MSFT FLIGHT SIMULATOR

### Common settlement and reserve model

Primary sources: [COINflip](https://docs.netnet.capital/coinflip), [Invaders](https://docs.netnet.capital/spacex-invaders), [Flight Simulator](https://docs.netnet.capital/flight-simulator).

All three are equity-native: stakes, bankroll and winnings are in COIN, SPCX, or MSFT respectively. USDG input converts the stake through Rialto; the fee is separated first. Losses join the Manager's house fund. A **5%-of-stake fee** splits equally between Manager revenue and the RWA Sleeve. Native-equity fees reach their corresponding recipients; USDG Sleeve fees convert at sweep. **No fee goes to the protocol Treasury; no bet touches NET.** The house bankroll is Manager capital, not RFV or protocol-owned liquidity.

Each accepted wager reserves its maximum payout. A placed reservation cannot be defunded by Manager withdrawal, while unreserved bankroll remains operational and withdrawable. Maximum reserved payout is **5% of free bankroll**, but the unit of the cap differs below. These protections are documented contract invariants, not independently verified solvency in this research.

The contracts name a drand quicknet round at least **15 seconds in the future**, verify the BLS signature, and permit settlement without operator exclusivity. New-entry halts do not govern already-placed settlement. The published outage rule permits only the bettor to void for **stake plus fee** when the named round remains unpublished two hours after its due time. Timing protections rely on sequencer timestamps within the stated skew assumptions; relay and settlement still require liveness. Do not reinterpret permissionless settlement as automatic settlement or operator prose as a bytecode audit.

Native-equity play is documented as 24/7. The direct USDG conversion gate requires an open equity session and a feed no older than four hours; the app describes a separate off-hours stock-acquisition route with wider spreads and possible oracle-deviation rejection. Thus “betting never blocked” is marketing shorthand, not an unconditional availability guarantee. Stock-to-USDG sell-back can queue until reopening and executes at the reopening price, exposing the holder to weekend gaps. The quoted stock-token calendar is Sunday 8 p.m. through Friday 8 p.m. Eastern; fresh venue/feed conditions take precedence.

### COINflip

Documented opening: 2026-08-10. One to four coins at launch, extensible to six by the Manager within the stated ceiling. The same selected side must appear on **every** coin. Win probability is `2^-N`, gross payout `2^N × stake`, so the table is exactly fair before its fee; more coins increase variance, not expected return. Four coins lose outright 15/16 of the time. The all-in return is about **95.2%** before conversion costs and equity moves.

The minimum is $1 all-in at the feed mark. Full payout reservation is capped at 5% of free bankroll **per bet and per named beacon round in aggregate**; the latter prevents many wagers stacking on a shared outcome. The page derives coin bits from the beacon signature without a per-bet salt, so do not assume same-round wagers are independent. The $20,000 launch seed and example maximums are historical; mode/minimum/halt settings and free coverage need fresh reads.

### SPACEX INVADERS

Documented opening: 2026-08-11. An eight-row reverse-Plinko path ends in nine buckets with binomial weights `1,8,28,56,70,56,28,8,1` out of 256. The launch multipliers are **25, 2.7, 1.2, 0.5, 0.2, 0.5, 1.2, 2.7, 25**. Their weighted sum is exactly 0.90 stake. A return below stake is common, not necessarily a zero payout; both 25× edge buckets together occur 2/256 of the time.

Up to 100 balls share one future round and a single settlement wait. A ball-index salt derives each path from the signature; ship aiming changes only animation. The source does not salt by batch id in its printed derivation, so independence of separate same-round batches is not established by that text. All-in minimum is $5 **per batch**, not per ball. Worst-case reservation is every ball hitting the top multiplier, constrained to 5% of free bankroll **per batch**; do not import COINflip's aggregate-round cap without a source. Active ladders may be retuned within the asserted return band for future batches. The $10,000 seed is a launch figure.

### MSFT FLIGHT SIMULATOR

Documented opening: 2026-08-15. A jump line **1.01×–16×** is committed before the result. If the random ceiling reaches it, the payout is that line times stake; otherwise the stake is lost. There is **no live manual cash-out** or pilot skill after placement: the flight animation replays the already-fixed result.

The survival curve is `P(C ≥ m) = 0.90 / m`. A 2× line wins 45%; a 16× line wins 5.625%; each has the same 90% expected gross stake return before fees. One in ten ceilings is below 1×. Ticket-id salting distinguishes flights sharing a beacon. Maximum payout is 5% of free bankroll **per ticket and per round in aggregate**. The app minimum is $5 all-in while the contract dust floor is $1. Manager withdrawal has an additional **2-MSFT nonzero operating floor**; complete wind-down to zero is separately allowed. The $10,000 seed is historical, not current coverage.

## TURBO — a leveraged instrument, not random odds

Primary source: [Long-Dated Desk](https://docs.netnet.capital/turbo). Documented opening: 2026-08-19. It belongs here because its cards are Blackjack chips and the arcade links to its separate application, not because it is a chance game.

An ERC-1155 series fixes stock token, financing level **K**, units per card, issuance capacity and expiry. A CALL has intrinsic value `units × max(mark − K, 0)`; a PUT has `units × max(K − mark, 0)`. The launch board lists NVDA/AAPL calls and puts at **25x**, placing CALL K 4% below and PUT K 4% above listing mark. Effective leverage changes as price moves; it is not maintained at 25x. A guard stops new sales below half the original effective leverage but not exits. The launch $5 sizing, 100 cards per series and 2026-09-18 expiry describe particular historical listings.

**One valid adverse touch kills a series permanently:** a CALL at/below K or PUT at/above K is knocked out, pays zero and never revives. There is no residual. Cards remain transferable even when worthless. A surviving expiry settles intrinsic at the first fresh in-session mark at/after expiry. The payoff is not a listed-company option; the source describes no time-value premium or daily funding. Its claims about fair intrinsic pricing are model claims, not a guaranteed profitable trade or verified market valuation.

**Collateral and denomination:** a CALL reserves full underlying stock units; a PUT reserves `units × K` USDG, sufficient even at zero stock mark. CALL payouts are stock tokens, PUT payouts USDG. Capacity is bounded by pre-funded Manager pots; unsold series capacity does not regenerate just because cards close. Knocks release reservation to those pots. Sleeve stock funding a house pot remains outside Treasury backing.

**Costs:** 5% of premium to open and 5% of net profit on a winning close/expiry, each split equally Manager/Sleeve; **1% buy markup and 1% cash-out markdown** accrue to the house pot. A transferred card has zero basis at the receiving wallet, so the source says 5% applies to its full proceeds. Secondary purchase cost is not automatically recognized by the desk. Knockout risk is additional to all fees paid.

**Price ladder, not universal instant liquidity:** fresh in-session Chainlink comes first; a sufficiently deep token pool's 30-minute average is an off-hours fallback; otherwise the last frozen close is used. Source launch examples say NVDA passed the depth bar and AAPL did not, which is not a current liquidity assertion. Off-hours mark bands of 0.20%/0.50% can be large relative to a leveraged card; haircut is capped at 25% of card value. Frozen-close purchases have a 25%-of-remaining-capacity per-session cap. A token-market barrier breach requires a second checkpoint ten minutes later and pauses buys/cash-outs while pending; the frozen rung cannot register a new knock. Fresh opening marks can expose a previously hidden adverse gap.

The docs' broad “always cashable” and “no exit halt” statements describe limits on **Manager authority**, not a waiver of pending-breach, feed, corporate-action or settlement preconditions. New listings require a fresh in-session mark. Fourteen days of in-session feed silence voids a series for net-premium refund in kind at the last mark; this is not intrinsic-value settlement. The [Blackjack disclosure](https://docs.netnet.capital/blackjack) adds multiplier-drift fail-closing and a grace-to-void process for its table series. A current series' end state and applicable generation must be resolved before discussing any claim value.

**September relaunch, not a current board:** on [September 9 at 19:04 UTC](https://x.com/NetNetCap/status/2097763186925834734), the publisher announced TURBO live again with **25x AAPL and SPCX LONG** cards and increased FY-HI liquidity; at [19:46 UTC](https://x.com/NetNetCap/status/2097773584106266872), an **NVDA 25x refill**. These supplement the original NVDA/AAPL listings. The [same-day roughly 170% APR claim](https://x.com/NetNetCap/status/2097783896012390502) concerns “Turbo Capital,” not guaranteed cardholder returns, sustainable rates or verified current earnings. “Cash out anytime” does not waive oracle, knock, capacity or settlement limits; current cards, collateral, liquidity, rates and acceptance remain unverified.

## TURBO BLACKJACK — gambling in leveraged chips

Primary sources: [Blackjack](https://docs.netnet.capital/blackjack) and [TURBO](https://docs.netnet.capital/turbo). The registry gives deployment 2026-08-20; the program page gives public opening 2026-08-22. These are different milestones, not necessarily conflicting dates.

The dedicated table series is a **5x NVDA CALL**, initially about $1 a chip, with K 20% below its listing mark. **Every player's and house's chip value can go to zero together while a hand is running.** More chips does not mean more dollar wealth if NVDA falls. A knock pays nothing; expiry and void are different: cards retain settlement/refund rights under the Long-Dated Desk rather than becoming economically worthless by definition. The cabinet returns cards for those latter outcomes. The stated roll policy expires the table series before NVDA's next ex-dividend date to avoid corporate-action voids; the current series and roll calendar require fresh evidence.

Rules: six decks, dealer stands on soft 17, blackjack 3:2, double any first two cards, double after split, up to four split hands; aces split once with one card each and no blackjack/resplit; no surrender or insurance; dealer peeks on ace/ten. Bets are even whole-chip counts, minimum two, so 3:2 pays exactly. The cut is 234 of 312 cards (75%).

**Do not collapse the costs:** the page reports **0.426% of initial bet** house edge against basic strategy from its simulation, plus a **1% deposit rake rounded up to a whole chip** per entry, split Manager/Sleeve. There is no per-hand fee, but rule edge accrues on wagering volume. Acquisition also pays TURBO opening fee/markup; liquidation bears TURBO's cash-out markdown and applicable profit fee. Won/transferred chips carry the documented zero-basis issue. The page's card-counting income illustration is a model, not a reliable income strategy; its statement that counting can overcome the rule edge does not eliminate entry costs or leveraged market loss.

**Randomness and hidden information:** each shoe combines a precommitted house secret, future drand signature, client entropy, and derived shoe id. Inputs and shoe ordering are fixed before the beacon arrives; the secret hides undealt cards until shoe-close publication enables reconstruction. A public beacon alone would expose the whole shoe. Pipelining removes later-shoe waits without making the house secret public early. This is a documented randomness guarantee, not full on-chain enforcement of every payout.

**Coverage and server risk:** the house float consists of the same cards as payouts. Held cards must cover session escrow, worst-case wins, rake and unclaimed amounts. Session payout cap is documented as five times deposited chips, no seat may reserve more than 10% of float, and hand sizing covers four doubled split hands. The example uses the post-rake stack for its roughly $465 cap; a precise cap calculation should be checked against the deployed generation rather than inferred from the prose wording. Market-free card transfers separate cabinet exit from the keeper's float rebalancing.

Hands run in an off-chain two-signature state channel. A bet is debited in a co-signed state before dealing. Cooperative settlement returns the latest position; unilateral close has a **one-hour challenge window** for a newer co-signed state. Sessions have a **four-hour deadline**, followed by a one-hour player-exclusive expiry window. The source explicitly says there is **no on-chain disputed-hand adjudicator**: a house refusing to sign the result can cost the player one hand's bet. Retaining/accessing the newest state and reacting to a stale close matters; “provably fair” does not erase this server/counterparty exposure.

Primary source: [Pooled-Distribution Desk](https://docs.netnet.capital/the-button). **The indexed page says not operating, built/rehearsed but not deployed, with no address.** The same-date arcade bundle has Button rules and halted/retired UI text. These document an app/docs gap, not a verified deployment, armed pot, retirement or reopening.

Training Grounds mechanics/current availability remain unestablished; the OpenSea-verification claim is distinct from [collection identity corroboration](nfts.md#collections).

The documented design charges $1 USDG per press, splits it **50% pot / 50% Sleeve** in NVDA, and grants permanent one-wallet-one-seat eligibility after the first settled press. More presses do not increase the equal-share weight; each instead buys another **1-in-2,000** chance to trigger the hit. The triggering presser gets **10% of converted NVDA pot**, with the other 90% equally credited to every eligible wallet including that presser. Claims are credited, not pushed, and do not expire. The press uses a future drand round; the source describes a presser-only refund for an unpublished round without providing a precise timeout here.

A hit empties and disarms the pot until a discretionary refill; the source illustrates **$1,000 NVDA per round** but promises neither future rounds nor continuing funding. A five-minute conversion duty is described as 24/7. Unconverted USDG is **not yet the winning pot**; timing and NVDA price changes affect the actual distribution. Pot funds cannot be withdrawn by the Manager under the described rules, but future refills are not a liability to participants or NET holders.

The source's “zero desk take” arithmetic is a **round-level expectation including the Manager refill**: at 2,000 expected presses, half of receipts equals the $1,000 seed. It is not zero cost per press, a guaranteed $1 return, or a Core revenue calculation. Permanent early seats benefit from later participants; many wallets can obtain more seats (Sybil dilution). Repeated presses add no ordinary weight. The tradeable Button Presser ERC-721 is explicitly distinct from permanent wallet eligibility: do not assume transferring the souvenir transfers the seat. The page's rough per-share example is not a stable payout because eligible-wallet count persists across rounds and differs from total presses.

## THE BOARD MEETING — counterparties choose, reward systems differ

Primary source: [Player-vs-Player Desk](https://docs.netnet.capital/the-board-meeting). Documented deployment/opening: 2026-09-02/03.

A $20 USDG entry is converted to MSFT: **$15 pot, $4 house, $1 jackpot**. House receipts split equally Manager/Sleeve; no automatic Core Treasury route is described. Two players therefore contest a $30 pot in a 30-second conversation/choice round with a five-second reveal window:

| Sealed choices | Ordinary pot result |
|---|---|
| SPLIT / SPLIT | Each receives $15-equivalent MSFT, a $5 shortfall against entry before rewards |
| STEAL / SPLIT | Thief receives the $30-equivalent pot; splitter receives none of that pot |
| STEAL / STEAL | Both receive zero; half the forfeited pot goes to jackpot, half to house |

Neither locked choice nor whether a choice was locked is disclosed to the other player before sealed revelation. An unchosen/default seat is treated as SPLIT **for money only**, with no loot roll or jackpot ticket. If neither locks, the table is void and no one is charged.

**Separate rewards:** an explicit SPLIT lock earns loot and a jackpot ticket even when robbed. A STEAL lock earns neither, but a successful steal alone has a **1-in-10,000 drand** chance at THE CASTING VOTE, the unique artifact. The jackpot ticket is **1 in 5,000**, whole pot to one winner. The documented launch loot table contains 1,060 finite pieces, per-piece chance 1/20,000 and roughly one drop per 19 rolls while full; depletion changes availability and ultimately closes that table. Loot minting depends on the player's table acknowledgement reaching the matchmaker; a missing acknowledgement leaves an outstanding on-chain claim according to the source, not an automatically delivered NFT.

The table outcome is **human choice, not a beacon draw**. Loot/jackpot derive from a three-party commitment using the house secret and both player salts. Only the 1/1 artifact is described as drand-provably-fair. Do not extend that specific label to matchmaking, monetary outcomes or all rewards.

The documentation models ~54% STEAL and about **31% house take / 69% player return** at equilibrium, versus ~24% STEAL observed on launch day. Its ~$34,000 equilibrium jackpot, daily frequency and ~$6.90 ticket EV are model/launch claims, not live odds of earning a dollar profit. The strategy mix, finite loot, jackpot timing and repeated-player behavior matter. A typical player missing jackpots can experience substantially worse returns (the source illustrates ~47% effective loss). “The house isn't your opponent” does not mean no house edge.

MSFT pots, fees and jackpot use stock units. Entry quotes apply the corporate-action multiplier and a 5% deviation gate. A plain seat close, jackpot claim or loot mint does not use an oracle; conversion to USDG is a separate operation that can fail or wait. The page specifically warns an MSFT stock split would stop new entries and cash-outs pending successor deployment, but not the in-kind seat close.

**Direct feed mark is not the entry quote.** The publisher says the entry adapter applies a multiplier, while Chainlink describes the catalogued equity feed as a total-return value. Explorer-indexed Boardroom bytecode contains the catalogued MSFT token/feed addresses and an adapter reference, but source bodies for Boardroom and that adapter were unavailable through the inspected explorer and source-recovery paths. The indexed MSFT token implementation distinguishes raw balances from multiplier-adjusted display balances; a multiplication is not by itself proof of double counting. Use the exact product's own read-only quote/oracle output with an established scale and unit basis, not a formula inferred from the direct feed directory. Current product arithmetic remains unverified. See [feed and raw/display unit guidance](addresses-and-roles.md#price-feeds-and-token-relationships).

The server matches players, times rounds and co-signs seat states. A newer co-signed state supports independent close; contested closes have a **one-hour challenge window**. An idle funded seat expires after 24 hours at last posted state. The documented payout bound is deposits plus half of cumulative stakes, or 50 times deposits, whichever is smaller. Daily cap is 50 tables per rolling day per character and wallet. Colluding players can guarantee handshakes and extract pooled rewards more efficiently; the source acknowledges other players bear that dilution. Server uptime, retained state and successful reward acknowledgement are operational dependencies, not solved by a fair artifact beacon.

## BASKETS — refereed competition, USDG bags and a separate stock purchase

Controlling source: [Grab Desk (BASKETS)](https://docs.netnet.capital/baskets), read 2026-09-18. It says operating/opened **2026-09-14**; [Official Channels](https://docs.netnet.capital/official-channels) also dates deployment to that day. These are publisher statements, not independently verified deployment or proof of present entry availability. Resolve BasketsDesk, the Permit2 entry spender and any later stock router separately through the [address index](../assets/address-index.json); the registry explicitly leaves the stock-buy router to Rialto's changeable on-chain registry.

**Allocation, not a promised return:** two to four players each contribute **$100 USDG** for a 60-second match. Each seat supplies **$25 opening bag, $50 in ten $5 falling baskets, and $25 house allocation**. Of that last $25, **$10 goes to the Manager, $10 to the RWA Sleeve and $5 to the jackpot**; the documentation says neither house bucket can be zeroed. Forty baskets/$300 on the floor are the **four-player example**, not a universal count. Untaken baskets also go to the jackpot, so the page's “players get back 75%” describes gross floor allocation before uncollected baskets, not guaranteed aggregate settlement or an individual $75 payout. A heavily robbed player can receive far less. No Core Treasury remittance or reserve backstop is specified.

**The floor and jackpot:** bags and steals are dollars, not moving stock marks; stock names on baskets are cosmetic. A steal moves $1 per accepted press. Five shake presses break a latch after an initial one-second lock; the latch times out after three seconds and the input ceiling is 133 milliseconds between presses. The referee runs 30 steps per second. Two or three queued players wait 20 seconds for more; the documented cap is 50 matches per wallet per rolling 24 hours. The gold basket carries one jackpot ticket: the first successful $1 theft, not merely a latch, transfers it. Its final holder gets a **1-in-5,000** whole-jackpot roll on a future public randomness round about 45 seconds after the bell. The docs describe no house jackpot-withdrawal function. Jackpot size, hit timing and payout are contingent, not fixed additional return.

**USDG-first settlement:** every bag is paid in USDG in one match settlement; no stock feed or swap is needed for that desk payout. The subsequent stock conversion is a separate wallet-originated purchase with a fresh Rialto quote and slippage bound, not the denomination of the match claim. It is optional/separable exposure: if the buy does not happen or fails, the player holds USDG rather than an unpaid bag. A stock purchase adds execution, market-hours, equity-price and issuer risks.

**Liveness and authorization limits:** the documented fallback permits self-settlement **15 seconds after the bell**, but requires access to the referee-signed result. If no settlement lands **120 seconds after match opening**, anyone may trigger a void/refund of the full **$100 including the house allocation**. The deadline creates a triggerable remedy, not evidence that an automatic refund transfer occurred; chain availability and a successful settlement/refund transaction still matter. The cabinet retains its last-match display for 24 hours. Queue authorization through Permit2 lasts **ten minutes**, and leaving the queue can leave residual exposure until expiry; leaving the screen is not proof that authorization has already ceased.

**Referee trust is not randomness:** only the committed drop seed mixed with a later public beacon and the jackpot's public-randomness roll carry the page's “provably fair” label. Gameplay is **refereed and replayable**. Publishing seed, salts and inputs supports accountability; it does not establish on-chain enforcement of every input or eliminate a dishonest signed result. The documentation expressly describes compromise of **both house and relayer keys plus the queue** allowing an attacker to pair a waiting authorization with their own wallet and mis-sign the result, extracting about **$50 per waiting player** while authorizations remain valid. Separate services and limited authorization lifetime constrain but do not remove that risk. Latency disadvantages slower players; neither the “house is not on the floor” slogan nor replayability guarantees uptime, correct settlement or favorable economics.

### Publisher rules and economic boundaries

- **The wager:** choose a side of a sports spread fixed when the board opens. Both sides pay even money before fees. Entries close at scheduled kickoff; no live betting or reopening for a delayed kickoff. The rules treat postponement as a push. This is a sports-score decision, not a drand lottery or Predict's weekly Treasury-plus-Sleeve outcome.
- **Risk off:** lock wsNET and wager only the realized rebases earned between placement and grade, subject to the reserved cover described by the client model. A win adds the wagered rebases again less the applicable fee; a loss gives up that yield while preserving the stated original **NET quantity**, not the original wsNET share count or USD value. Placement quotes and kickoff rebase counters are estimates, not the realized wager. A later grade can include additional rebases and change the economic outcome; a projected staking rate is not guaranteed yield.
- **Risk on:** principal is at risk. The fee is deducted when the bet is placed; a win credits twice the **net wager**, while a loss forfeits it. **The placement fee is retained on a push or void.** “Every bet refunded” must therefore not be reported as a full gross-payment refund for risk-on bets.
- **Balances and locks:** the publisher distinguishes free wsNET balance from locked wagers and risk-off principal. Free balance is described as withdrawable; locked amounts await grading/void and settlement. Graded, claimable, settled into free balance, and actually withdrawn are different states. A grade alone is not evidence that a wallet received a payout.
- **Grade and liveness:** the rules assign final-score posting to the **team Safe**, no earlier than **three hours after kickoff**, with the result derived from score and fixed line. If ungraded after **72 hours**, anyone may void; void settles as a push. These are **publisher rules, not audited enforcement** or proof of current grader authority. Permissionless voidability is a transaction-dependent remedy, not an automatic refund. Grader timing affects realized rebase exposure even if the score is correct.
- **Fee and house:** the stated baseline is **3% of wager**, increasing toward **6%** when adding imbalance at the coverage limit and decreasing toward **1.5%** when reducing imbalance; the rate is fixed at placement. Each fee splits **half to the house pot, half to the RWA Sleeve**. The publisher calls the Sleeve the house and explicitly excludes the Treasury; house capital, reserved cover, realized house P&L and fee receipts are different quantities. No Core reserve backstop or NET-holder distribution follows.
- **Funding and valuation:** the book holds, wagers and settles **wsNET**. USDG entry buys NET on the canonical pair, pays **5% trading tax plus pool fee/slippage**, then stakes/wraps; USDG exit sells NET and pays **5% again**, plus pool fee/slippage. The rules label direct wsNET/sNET entry and exit untaxed. Approximate dollar displays use the sNET index and canonical NET pair spot at read time; that is neither fixed USD protection nor a separately verified USDG/USD mark.

**Push/void matrix — published client model, not verified contract enforcement:**

| Mode | Credit on push or void after settlement | Fee treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Risk on | Fee-net wsNET wager returned, not the original gross debit | Placement fee remains charged |
| Risk off | Original locked wsNET amount returned | No wager-fee subtraction in the client model |

These are internal account credits, not proof of an external withdrawal. Graded-but-unsettled amounts remain distinct from settled credits; a push/void is not a losing wager even when retained risk-on fees produce a negative net result. Positive rebases can change the NET equivalent of returned wsNET. Verify actual settlement events, credits and fee/transfer legs before claiming enforced outcomes.

“No loss” is native-NET principal framing for **risk off**, not a guarantee of dollars, uninterrupted withdrawals, rebase income, audited solvency or contract safety. NET price declines, foregone staking yield, locks, conversion costs, grading discretion, chain/wrapper/contract failures and jurisdictional eligibility remain relevant. Risk on can lose principal. The September 18 casino and self-repaying-loan teaser is not made live by this sports-book launch; see [roadmap distinctions](builders.md#keep-the-announced-concepts-separate).
